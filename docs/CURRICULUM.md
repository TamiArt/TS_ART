# TS·ART Studio — Curriculum

This programme map now has an authored first-draft lesson set in the app: 20 modules and 84 lessons total (8 in Module 1, 4 in each other module). The sequence and lesson count are a working draft; review with beginners and qualified art educators is still required.

## Current implementation status\n\n- All 20 modules are interactive in the app.\n- Module 7 includes a Colour Lab sequence and the app has a separate local experiment journal.\n- Lesson text is a first draft, not yet validated by learner research or expert review.\n- Progress and notes are browser-local; there is no account or cloud sync.\n\n## Programme map

## Module 1 — first production milestone

Build this module in full before scaling the entire curriculum. Proposed contents:
- 8–12 complete lessons.
- 20–30 mini-studio exercises.
- A baseline diagnostic that is exploratory rather than a high-stakes test.
- A basic practice journal.
- Clear self-check criteria.
- A short review sequence.
- A first piece of work to compare against later practice.

Potential lesson topics:
1. Seeing before drawing.
2. Setting up a workable practice space.
3. Materials: essential versus optional.
4. Observation and proportion diagnostic.
5. Line, shape, and negative space.
6. Light and dark: first value observations.
7. Describing what is seen rather than what is assumed.
8. Recording decisions and questions.
9. Reviewing work without vague self-criticism.
10. Setting a sustainable practice routine.

These are proposed topics; sequence and exact lesson count should be validated.

## Teaching pattern across modules

Every module should include:
- A clear introduction and expected outcomes.
- A visual vocabulary list.
- Demonstrations and worked examples.
- Focused short practices.
- A larger task.
- Common errors and correction strategies.
- A self-check rubric.
- A transfer task.
- A reflection or portfolio record.
- Review links to previously introduced skills.

## Cross-module links

- Construction and proportion support still life, landscape, figure, portrait, and digital painting.
- Value and edges support every painting medium.
- Colour relationships lead into the pigment lab, landscape, sky, water, portrait, and master studies.
- Perspective and composition are revisited in every author project.
- Art history should be connected to actual visual analysis throughout the course, not isolated as names and dates.
- Traditional and digital media should share core principles while acknowledging their different materials, tools, and constraints.

## Review and progression

Use the shared skill states from the master plan: Introduced → With support → Independent → Stable in a new task. Learners may revisit modules. Progress should be demonstrated through work and transfer tasks rather than inferred solely from watching a video.
